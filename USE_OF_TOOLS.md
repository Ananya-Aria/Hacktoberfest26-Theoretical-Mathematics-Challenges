# Use of tools

Please read this before you start. It matters more than any single task.

## Why we're asking

This repository isn't about collecting as many pull requests as possible. It's a chance to see what the Theoretical and Mathematical Committee is about, the kind of problems we enjoy, the things and projects we will tackle in, and maybe to figure out what you're interested in. That only works if the thinking is yours.

You do **not** need to know how to code. Pseudocode, reasoning and plain English are just as welcome.

## The order we'd like you to follow

1. **Try it yourself first.** Give each task real time. Wrong guesses and messy attempts are part of the work, not a failure.
2. **Talk to people.** Ask in the Enigma group, or discuss with friends. Discussing ideas is encouraged, and often more fun than solving alone. Then write your own solution, in your own words.
3. **Tools only if you're genuinely stuck.** If you've tried and talked and are still stuck, you may ask an AI tool for a *hint*, not the answer. Be careful: these tasks have details that tools often get wrong, so check everything they tell you.

## What's fine and what isn't

| Fine                                                     | Not fine                                               |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Asking the group or a friend for a hint                  | Copying someone else's solution                        |
| Asking an AI what a word like "Average estimation" means | Pasting a task into an AI and copying the answer       |
| Asking an AI for a small hint after trying for a while   | Asking an AI to write your explanations                |
| Checking your own arithmetic after you've done it        | Letting a tool do your tables, traces or walks for you |
| Asking anyone (or any llm) for help with git errors      | Submitting code or solutions you can't explain         |

## The rules

1. Everything you submit must be written by you, in your own words.
2. All the work that uses your own Enigma number (your map, your tables, your traces) must be done by you.
3. At the end of your submission, say who and what helped you: people you discussed with, websites, AI tools, and what for. "None" is a valid answer.
4. You must be able to **explain every line** of your submission. Reviewers may ask you about your answer in the pull request or in person. If you can't explain it, it won't be merged.
5. At the end, the final call will remain in the hands of the maintainers, so please follow this, cus if you don't, we might know 👀

If something is pasted straight from an AI without any effort of your own, it usually shows. Please don't put us, or yourself, in that position.

Have a lovely Hacktober 💙
